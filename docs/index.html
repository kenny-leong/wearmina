The problem

"Where's that blazer from?" shouldn't be homework.

People want the outfit the second they see it. When Queen of Tears aired, fashion blogs went through Hong Hae-in's wardrobe episode by episode, and every one of those posts started with somebody pausing the show to go looking.

Today

  1. Pause the episode.
  2. Try to screenshot it. Get a black rectangle, because Netflix blocks that.
  3. Google "Hae-in red blazer episode 7".
  4. Dig through a fashion blog until you find someone who worked it out.
  5. Learn it's Balmain and it's $2,595. Close the tab.

And that's when it goes well. Plenty of outfits never get identified at all.

With Mina

Tap the blazer.

That's it. You get the exact piece, plus a few that look like it for a lot less, and you never leave the show.

      How it works

      How it knows what she's wearing

      Four things happen between your tap and the product page. Click one to see what it does.

      Platforms

      Wherever you watch

      It's the same catalog behind all three. Hit a button to see that version in the demo.

      Browser extension

      An extension for Chrome, Safari and Edge that puts the tags right on the player. We'd build this first, because it doesn't need a deal with Netflix or anyone else.

      Smart TV + phone

      Nobody wants to shop with a TV remote. So the TV shows one small prompt, and the looks turn up on your phone after a quick QR scan.

      Mobile

      Watching on your phone? The looks slide up under the video, so you can browse with one thumb and keep the show on.

      Business model

      How it makes money

      Viewers never pay. Brands pay when a scene sells something, and some will pay just to find out which scenes do.

      Per sale

      Affiliate commission

      When someone buys through a tag, the brand pays Mina a cut. It's a normal affiliate link that happens to start on a TV screen.

      Per placement

      Featured looks

      Sometimes the real piece is custom, or sold out years ago. A brand can pay to be the suggested alternative, and it's always marked as featured.

      Per seat

      Insights subscription

      A dashboard for brands and studios that shows which scenes, characters and pieces people tap on, episode by episode.

      Where this goes

      1. Now

        Concept prototype

        This page. You can click through the whole idea, but none of it is real yet.

      2. Pilot
